Stewart's longer-term development should let the user commission substantial
changes through one high-level agent and remain involved chiefly in purpose,
semantics and the assessment of results. The agent owns the organization of
labor and makes its results intelligible.

Use a recurring pattern when it fits the work:

1. **Inquiry:** establish a concrete question, investigate the implementation
   and propose the behavior worth pursuing.
2. **Semantic audit:** examine existing responsibilities and decide which
   should survive, change or end.
3. **Optional addition:** consider new responsibilities, including the
   possibility that none is justified.
4. **Tool transition:** establish the implementation foundation appropriate
   to the selected semantics.
5. **Optional implementation:** realize the changes the user chooses to
   commission, with evidence that they meet their intended responsibilities.

This pattern can evolve. A stage can end in a decision, a reframed question or
a reason to stop. Inquiry and semantic decisions need not force immediate
implementation, and a technical migration does not automatically authorize a
larger custody role. A bounded implementation change may support an inquiry
when independently useful; an intermediate tool migration is not a required
disciplining exercise before the selected transition.

Human-readable documents are our shared account of direction and progress.
Usually we check in at the level of stage purpose, intended behavior,
consequential alternatives, evidence, integrated outcomes and open questions.
Go deeper when a decision needs it. Keep the account short enough to use and
revise it as understanding changes.

Keep the whole forward program recoverable as well as each decision. The
[development plan](DEVELOPMENT-PLAN.md) owns the dependency sequence, outstanding
work and evidence needed to finish it. Before dispatching workers, identify
shared interfaces, exclusive edit ownership, integration order and external
dependencies; do not make the user manage those logistics. An accepted change
stays visible until implemented and verified or given an explicit disposition.
Update the current account after integration rather than accumulating competing
checkpoints. Prepare future waves concretely without treating planning as a
commission to run them.

The coordinator's operating guidance now lives in
[coordination/](coordination/README.md). Its standing orders own dispatch,
capacity, permission and recovery practice; the working account records current
work. One coordinator owns the coherent result at a time, including across
harness changes. Each coordinator keeps durable guidance and current accounts
updated as work proceeds. Before a planned transfer the user specifically directs
a thin turnover briefing; the incoming coordinator reads it, reconciles its useful
content and stubs it. Work saved during the session supports an abrupt departure
without a fresh brief. This keeps turnover from becoming a growing runbook. This
vision supplies the reasons for the arrangement rather than a second set of
standing procedures.

Judge the arrangement by coherent, verified work and reduced coordination
burden while the user retains semantic control. More agents or more machinery
are not success measures. Improve the practice through experience; identify
lasting procedural changes explicitly rather than accumulating them in turnover
prose. Coordinator judgments remain attributable beside their subject.

Keep this development arrangement distinct from the product relationship.
Stewart's intended users want narrow questions and answers; a bounded
commission and structured return are a positive choice. The steering and
supervision available to a development coordinator are not a product roadmap.
Judge additional intervention facilities by a specific commission or custody
need. In particular, a hold must not extend the investigation or excuse
unfinished operations after close. Elaborate facilities during a hold are a
reason to re-examine the commission, capture and closure boundaries first.

Treat the user's preference for at most one audit retry facility, potentially
none, as a reason to reassess whole workflows. Explore the audit as a single
undertaking first: commission records supply provenance without inherently
promising replay, and the manager owns constructing another question.
Existing implementation effort does not settle which responsibilities belong
in Stewart. That review has now led to the decision to omit a dedicated program
executor from the successor, including its staged manager-operated release/run
workflow. Permitted auditor tool use remains; its reported execution results
retain their attribution. Carry the simplification through the dependent plans
without creating an external replacement service. Incomplete Stewart custody
operations still need supported handling without requiring an agent-manager to
manipulate private state. Fewer retries must not mean less responsibility for
what Stewart has already undertaken. The coordinator recommends zero audit
restarts for the first successor and schedules no convenience while the return
and custody interfaces remain unsettled. The possible restart interface remains
an open product choice, rather than an implementation prerequisite.

The user's 2026-10-27 lean is to find cases where inexpensive assurance lets
competent managers make their own determinations. Favor bounded handoffs through
trap doors with clear receipts. The intended everyday relationship is: the
manager gives Stewart copies of materials it is responsible for, immediately
receives detailed receipts, and eventually receives a result or error. Aim for
audits to become routine, with less runtime machinery and less managerial
coordination. Those benefits remain hypotheses to assess, not measured gains or
grounds for weakening custody obligations.

Commission recall is the concrete first application. Present the receipt and its
access route consistently enough that a manager who forgets the supplied question
or material can inspect the original undertaking without observing the auditor's
work. Do not turn occasional forgetting into a general memory-management service:
losing both the receipt/reference and knowledge of the standard route may require
more work. The manager still owns its competing inquiry and judgment. CONTRACTS.md
records this successor requirement and the broader lean; the development plan
owns its remaining presentation, custody and qualification decisions.

Develop the finite answer boundary with custody beginning during authoring of
recorded elements. Auditor submission or manager collection fixes the return
selection; the manager receives an independent durable copy with the original
commission context and provenance. Empty and partial returns use that same
interface with an honest account of gaps. Affirmative close follows the manager's
decision that the copy is safely placed, subject to remaining custody obligations.

The keyhole candidate's proposed disposability of other audit working state
remains a conditional design question. When affected preservation or disposal work
brings that choice into scope, examine what obligations it could eliminate or
narrow without weakening the field of investigation. A finite return selection
does not itself authorize disposal. Any retirement requires qualified preservation
and control; the candidate neither silently repeals existing preservation
obligations nor applies new rules to historical audits.

The established question types were learned through real audits. Give answer
structures comparable empirical attention: inspect actual returns, compare
representations generally and per question, then test fresh authoring through
a replaceable submission prototype. Stewart remains agnostic about the
intellectual utility of audits, including during development. Its major
intellectual influence belongs in question types and the constrained audit
space; assess how answer restrictions preserve, obscure or steer the responses
those questions permit. Agreement with a preferred outcome is not success.
Restrictions that privilege substantive outcomes require explicit deliberation.
Select the durable interface only after those comparisons, and enable automatic
disposal only after integrated custody and control qualification. Schema
validity alone establishes neither faithful expression nor inspectable support.
A possible new question type has its own inquiry.

Keep investigative outcome, submission admission, delivery and closure distinct
without turning them into manager-operated stages. A portable result still
serves a reader whose original environment is gone, but the earlier Python-
package reader is a bounded legacy candidate rather than the default successor
result model. The return route and launch/control arrangement require actual
qualification. The user selected bounded commission-input acceptance and its
independent verified readback as the first usable successor leg. Settle its
acceptance promise and outstanding custody responsibilities explicitly; a separate
reader preview is neither selected nor a prerequisite. Keep its claims to the
original undertaking. A genuine subsequent self-audit, preserved through an
existing responsible custody process, supplies experience for any later result
feature. Observe the investigator's needs without predetermining a new reader or
making successful result inspection an acceptance criterion for commission intake.
Close still follows qualified preservation and control.

The user strongly prefers one manager-facing `close` command that closes an
audit from any state. Design the stopping, partial-work accounting and custody
steps within that operation, including any internal withdrawal path. The
manager should not need to diagnose a stage and choose a separate closure
procedure. Existing obligations still constrain completion; an unfinished close
needs a supported continuation through the same entry point. Exact mechanics
and any concrete conflict with protected obligations remain for design and
decision.

Carry the product tension the user recorded on 2026-10-25 through these
inquiries: investigative effectiveness favors seemingly transparent access to
the manager-selected materials used to construct the question; seamless
operation favors a constrained, static slice without nesting or recursive
expansion. Their tradeoffs can be unexpected. Assess what a simplification
does to the auditor's ability to investigate as well as to the precision and
cost of Stewart's promises. Neither smaller scope nor broader access is a
success measure by itself. A narrow question may require rich selected context;
the question's boundary and the size of its evidence are different choices.
The product principles live in CONTRACTS.md.

Maintain this vision while the practice develops. When it has become ordinary
working guidance, move what remains useful into that guidance and retire this
plan.
