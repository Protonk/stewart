# Using Stewart's Five-Stage Development Program to Transition to Agent Swarm Logistics

## Purpose

Use Stewart's five-stage development program to change not only the software but the way its development is organized.

Move away from personally coordinating agent dispatch, handoffs, task decomposition, and integration. Delegate those responsibilities to a frontier agent that serves as your principal interface with the development program, the repository, and a self-organizing swarm of development agents.

Retain authority over Stewart's intended character, the scope of consequential changes, and the decisions that warrant your judgment. Relinquish routine control over how the work is distributed and executed.

Treat this transition as an evolving practice rather than a new orchestration system that must be designed and implemented before development can proceed.

## 1. Establish the frontier agent's responsibility

Give the frontier agent responsibility for translating your high-level objectives into coordinated development work.

Authorize it to investigate the repository, formulate plans, enlist subagents, distribute tasks, reconcile findings, oversee implementation, arrange review, and integrate completed work. Let it determine how much delegation is appropriate, including when an assignment is better performed directly.

Do not prescribe a fixed number of agents, a permanent division of labor, or a mandatory sequence of agent interactions.

Keep your own involvement concentrated on setting direction, resolving significant uncertainty about Stewart's intended behavior, authorizing changes to that behavior, and assessing the program's overall progress.

Require the frontier agent to distinguish questions that demand your authority from questions it can resolve through ordinary engineering judgment.

### Make coordination portable across harnesses

Allow work to proceed through both Codex and Claude. Treat their available capabilities, operating conditions, and compute allotments as resources to be managed, not as competing project structures.

Encourage the frontier agent to balance work across the available harnesses without introducing a mandatory pause whenever the preferred provider changes. Accommodate session boundaries while preserving continuity of assignments, decisions, and results.

Do not assume that an entire development stage belongs to a particular provider. Permit differences in strengths, costs, and availability to influence individual assignments.

Avoid creating coordination overhead merely to achieve symmetrical provider usage. Allocate work according to its needs and the resources available.

## 2. Protect semantic continuity without freezing Stewart

Before substantial changes, establish a working distinction among three categories:

**Foundational distinctions:** Properties of Stewart's intended character that should remain intact unless explicitly reconsidered.

**Contested custody and control arrangements:** Responsibilities, restrictions, permissions, and refusal behaviors whose necessity or desirability the program is authorized to investigate.

**Incidental implementation behavior:** Details that may change freely when doing so does not alter an intended contract.

Use Stewart's Core Facts as a starting point, not as proof that the present implementation behaves correctly or that every existing arrangement deserves preservation.

Pay particular attention to distinctions between commission and execution, custody and correctness, permission and availability, completion and substantive answer, and successful work and recoverable failure.

Maintain a lightweight semantic decision record. Have the frontier agent record which behaviors are intended to survive, which remain under examination, which changes have been authorized, and which questions are unresolved.

Keep that record sufficiently concise that it supports decisions rather than becoming a second repository to maintain.

## 3. Use the five stages as the development program

### Stage 1 — Move selectively to Click

Use the Click transition to expose the boundary between general-purpose command-line infrastructure and Stewart-specific behavior.

Delegate identification of suitable migration targets, implementation planning, execution, and verification to the frontier agent and its chosen subagents.

Preserve intended external behavior while permitting incidental implementation changes. Avoid investing heavily in Python infrastructure whose likely replacement by Rust would make the work wasteful.

Use this stage as the first substantial exercise in autonomous coordination. Evaluate whether the frontier agent can organize concurrent work, maintain a coherent integration process, and manage provider resources without requiring you to control individual assignments.

Do not make perfect orchestration a prerequisite for completing the stage.

### Stage 2 — Conduct the refusal audit

Commission a systematic examination of Stewart's existing custody and control arrangements.

Ask which restrictions and responsibilities should be hardened, softened, retained, or discarded. Require the investigation to consider what each mechanism protects, what obligations it creates, what costs it imposes, and what might happen if it changes.

Allow the swarm to divide the inquiry along whatever boundaries prove intellectually useful. Encourage independent examination when important conclusions are uncertain or disputed.

Do not require the investigation to defend the existing implementation. Equally, do not assume that simpler or less restrictive behavior is necessarily preferable.

Separate investigative findings from authorized semantic decisions. Review the consequential alternatives yourself, informed by the frontier agent's recommendations.

**Finish this stage with a reasoned account of what Stewart should retain, revise, or abandon—not with a requirement to implement every decision immediately.**

### Stage 3 — Consider additions to custody

Commission a complementary inquiry into responsibilities Stewart does not presently undertake but might reasonably assume.

Ask the frontier agent to examine potential additions in light of Stewart's purpose, the existing custody model, and the conclusions of the refusal audit.

For each proposed responsibility, determine what Stewart would undertake, what authority it would require, what evidence or commitments it would need to preserve, and how the responsibility could be discharged.

Permit the conclusion that a proposed addition is unnecessary, excessive, or outside Stewart's proper scope.

Decide the intended semantics before assigning implementation work.

Consolidate the results of Stages 2 and 3 into a coherent account of Stewart's desired custody arrangement. Distinguish decisions ready for implementation from ideas that remain exploratory.

### Stage 4 — Transition to Rust

Use the Rust migration to establish a new implementation foundation against an explicit understanding of Stewart's intended behavior.

Give the frontier agent substantial freedom to propose the migration sequence, divide implementation work, and determine suitable verification strategies.

Require it to distinguish regressions from deliberate departures. Do not treat every observed Python behavior as a requirement merely because it can be reproduced.

Preserve the relevant foundational distinctions and the contracts designated for continuity. Where an intended behavior is still awaiting implementation under Stage 5, make that status explicit rather than obscuring it within the language migration.

Avoid making the Rust transition dependent on first implementing every newly authorized custody responsibility in Python.

Assess the integrated Rust system rather than relying solely on the success of individual agent assignments.

### Stage 5 — Optionally implement a new custody arrangement in Rust

After the semantic decisions have been made, decide whether to commission implementation of the revised custody model in Rust.

Treat this as a distinct, optional stage. The Rust transition should not automatically authorize every contemplated expansion or redesign of Stewart's responsibilities.

When proceeding, supply the frontier agent with the authorized semantic decisions and delegate the organization of implementation and verification.

Require the swarm to demonstrate that the new behavior corresponds to the intended custody arrangement, rather than merely that the software operates successfully.

Preserve the ability to decline, narrow, postpone, or reconsider individual additions without disrupting the broader development program.

## 4. Let the development process evolve across the stages

Use the five stages as successive opportunities to increase the frontier agent's operational independence.

Begin with enough oversight to identify where your existing coordination practices contain important judgment that has not yet been articulated. When that judgment can be delegated safely, delegate it.

Do not respond to every coordination failure by adding another permanent procedure. Determine whether the failure calls for clearer authority, better records, a different division of work, or simply an exercise of judgment by the frontier agent.

Let the agent develop and revise its approach to dispatch, review, integration, and provider allocation as experience accumulates.

Avoid imposing a comprehensive swarm architecture at the outset. Prefer a small number of durable operating expectations over a large catalog of procedures.

Maintain a clear separation between the program's objectives and the methods agents choose to pursue them.

## 5. Require accountability without micromanagement

Ask the frontier agent to maintain an intelligible account of the work being undertaken, the important decisions reached, and the evidence supporting significant conclusions.

Expect regular summaries at meaningful milestones rather than exhaustive transcripts of agent interactions.

Have those summaries identify substantive progress, integrated results, important disagreements, outstanding risks, and decisions requiring your attention.

Make failures and incomplete investigations visible. Accept that some inquiries will properly conclude by requesting additional evidence or recommending that a question be reframed.

Do not equate agent activity with progress, successful integration with semantic correctness, or a completed assignment with resolution of the original question.

Review work at the level of outcomes and responsibilities. Intervene in the internal organization of the swarm when there is a concrete reason, not simply because an agent has chosen a different decomposition than you would have chosen.

## 6. Manage the program through consequential decisions

Concentrate your attention at a small number of decision points.

At the outset, establish the scope of delegation and the architectural commitments that must remain recognizable.

During the Click transition, assess whether the developing coordination process is sufficiently effective to support larger changes.

At the conclusion of the refusal investigations, determine Stewart's intended custody and control semantics.

Before and during the Rust migration, resolve questions about what behavior must survive and what may be discarded.

Before Stage 5, decide which new responsibilities merit implementation.

Allow the frontier agent to request additional decisions when an unforeseen issue exceeds its authority. Do not require your approval for every design choice, dispatch, or implementation step.

## 7. Judge the transition by the independence it produces

Evaluate the development process alongside the software it produces.

Ask whether the frontier agent can maintain a coherent understanding of the program, coordinate work across different harnesses, manage compute constraints, and integrate results without relying on you to broker communication among agents.

Look for a reduction in your involvement in operational details without a corresponding loss of semantic control.

Treat unnecessary coordination, lost context, duplicated work, poorly grounded decisions, and avoidable escalation as opportunities to improve the arrangement.

Resist measuring success by the number of agents recruited, the amount of parallel activity, or the sophistication of the orchestration machinery.

The desired outcome is a development process in which you can commission substantial transformations, exercise judgment at the level of purpose and semantics, and receive coherent, verified results.

## Governing principle

**Delegate the organization of labor much more aggressively than the authority to redefine Stewart.**

Use the Click migration to develop the working relationship, the refusal audit and addition stage to decide what Stewart should become, the Rust transition to establish its new implementation foundation, and the optional fifth stage to realize a newly authorized custody arrangement.

Let the agent swarm determine how best to perform the work. Require the frontier agent to make that work intelligible to you without making you responsible for coordinating it.

The five-stage program should produce both a transformed Stewart and a development practice capable of undertaking transformations of comparable scale with substantially less direct supervision.
