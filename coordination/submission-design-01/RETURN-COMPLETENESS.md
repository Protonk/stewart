# What complete expected material means

Discussion briefing, 2026-10-27. The user approved the recommendations about
finite return contents/destination, empty/partial return through the same interface,
and an unfinished close arranging receipt before the manager's final decision.
[CONTRACTS](../../CONTRACTS.md#successor-manager-receipt-and-ordinary-closure)
records those directions. The user then asked for specificity about the remaining
case: “when Stewart cannot supply the complete expected material.” No runtime
implementation or exact loss-disposition mechanism is commissioned here.

## The undertaking, not the hoped-for answer

Expected material means the finite package owed for one identified submitted or
manager-collected return: original question/instructions, commission receipt,
sealed manifest, every selected artifact's exact bytes, and provenance identifying
the audit and whether the auditor submitted or the manager collected it. It does
not mean every useful finding, a satisfactory answer, every workspace file, all
commission inputs by implication, or everything referenced in those documents.

A complete return requires a verifiable selection and binding, all required bytes,
and a completed independent manager-held copy. A failure to establish any of
these now is not necessarily permanent loss. Stewart must distinguish what it
cannot identify, what it cannot read or verify, and what it has not yet copied.
The manager's later judgment that its copy is safely placed remains separate.

For example, suppose an intact sealed manifest selects `answer.md`,
`measurements.csv` and `check.py`. Alongside that selection the package owes the
question, commission receipt and provenance. These cases have different meanings:

| Observation | What Stewart can and cannot claim |
| --- | --- |
| `measurements.csv` is missing or cannot be read. | Its required identity is known, but those bytes cannot currently be supplied. Missing and unreadable retain their different diagnostics. |
| That file exists but does not match the manifest's digest. | The observed bytes are not verified as the selected member. Copying them cannot satisfy the promise to supply that member. |
| The manifest or required identity/provenance record is missing, invalid or inconsistent. | Stewart may be unable to establish which selection is owed. Available blobs alone do not establish membership; unknown inventory is not an empty selection or a complete return. |
| Every source member verifies, but destination copying stops on a full disk or access failure. | Source custody may be intact while delivery is incomplete. Supported continuation must account for that copy and its partial outputs. |
| A verified copy and completion record exist, but the reply was lost. | Copying may already be complete. Reconcile the same undertaking and supply its result; a missing reply proves neither missing material nor receipt by the manager. |
| All artifact files arrive, but the original question or required commission binding cannot be supplied. | The package is incomplete even though its answer-file selection arrived. |

These are design cases, not observations of failures in a production successor.
The bounded prototype checked some publication/dependency distinctions; it did
not qualify manager copying or closing. Its relevant result is that damage to an
unrelated attempt or unused preparation need not invalidate an otherwise verified
current selection. Required dependencies still matter.

## Distinctions to preserve

- Early collection or an auditor stopping without submission can still yield a
  complete copy of the verifiably recorded selection, even an empty one. Missing
  intellectual work does not make the custody copy incomplete.
- A pending add needs same-operation accounting. No reply does not mean nothing
  was published; leftover staging bytes do not by themselves establish membership.
- Replaced/deleted versions are not current selected members merely because their
  payloads remain internally retained until close. Their separate custody/hold
  obligations remain; this package definition does not authorize their removal.
- Work never recorded through the helper is not automatically a sealed member.
  Any separate partial-work or workspace-preservation undertaking still needs its
  own accounting; a narrow return definition cannot silently erase it.
- If the manager later moves or loses a verified delivered copy, that does not
  retroactively change the earlier delivery observation. Another copy is supported
  before close; disposed audit material is not promised afterward.

## What remains to decide inside close

The approved direction already supplies verifiable available material and an
explicit gap account without another manager-facing triage stage. The unresolved
policy is the authority and record needed to end a still-unfulfilled undertaking.
An acknowledgment that a warning was seen is not necessarily authority to stop
trying to deliver named material and remove its remaining custody sources.

The coordinator recommends starting with the narrow known-selection case:
Stewart identifies the unsupplied member and its expected identity, what it
actually observed, what was copied, and the obligation that closing would end.
The manager could then choose supported completion or explicitly authorize that
named incomplete disposition within close. This is a recommendation for discussion,
not an adopted new mechanism. The loss remains recorded despite the undertaking;
it is never relabeled an opportunity the manager declined.

Unverifiable selection identity is a harder separate case: Stewart cannot ask the
manager to acknowledge an exact missing list that Stewart cannot establish. The
scope and authority of any disposition must state that uncertainty. A hold,
another lifetime's use or unproved stopping still has its own constraint; a
return-loss acknowledgment does not automatically resolve it.
