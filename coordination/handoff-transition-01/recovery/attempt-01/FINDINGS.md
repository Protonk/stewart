## Finding and recommendation

The proposed arrangement can support intake without a final message or turnover
brief. Its indispensable element is the account maintained during work, including
coordinator work and uncertain effects, with direct routes to briefs, checkpoints
and evidence. An optional brief must add orientation only. Standing orders should
explain how to reconcile those materials; they cannot establish that the last
coordinator or a worker has actually stopped.

Keep `coordination/COORDINATOR-NOTES.md` as the stable current account and dispatch
index if practical. This avoids changing the already integrated findings and
preserved completed outputs that link it. Give `coordination/` a durable landing
page or standing-orders entry, and choose one predictable optional turnover path.
The names are coordinator choices; do not preserve two live substantive handoffs.

## Minimum useful contents

Standing orders need only the coordinator role/authority boundary, intake route,
ownership of current account versus program plans/contracts/findings, persistent
checkpoint/dispatch/recovery practice, current permission constraints, and the
rule that turnover may be blank/missing or stale. Preserve the existing soft
capacity preference, bounded Claude-permission rule, evidence limits and user
ownership of purpose/protected obligations. Do not turn every inherited caution
or example into a new duty. General intake can say to discover active plans by
naming convention rather than embedding a particular active-plan route in durable
AGENTS or README guidance.

The current account needs the present commission and its limits, dated observed
state and pending next action, locators for active plans/dispatch index/evidence,
what was integrated versus merely returned, and consequential interpretation or
unresolved decisions beside their subject. For interrupted coordinator work it
also needs the admitted source/uncommitted inputs, owned shared edit paths,
work underway and uncertain commands/effects. These can be concise entries and
links to an existing commission/progress record; no new process-state service
or dedicated judgment file is needed. Keep completed work discoverable without
letting old “next action” paragraphs compete with the current commission.

A nonempty turnover can carry a date/revision, a few immediate observations,
cautions or intent, and links to authoritative work records. It is a selection
by the outgoing coordinator, not the only record of authority, unfinished work,
side effects or evidence. Its absence needs no repair before intake. Staleness
is established by comparison with the current work and sources, not its date
alone; a recent brief can still omit later changes.

## Limits and integration cautions

Repository instructions make the new entry discoverable through AGENTS and its
tracked CLAUDE alias. Whether each external harness actually presents those
instructions at session start is outside this source review. No private harness
configuration or session store was read, and no automatic-loading or fault
recovery behavior was qualified. An incoming coordinator still needs a repository
entry point; this transformation cannot prove every harness supplies it.

Retire the old root handoff only after all active substantive routes are fixed
and useful context has moved to its owner. Prefer deletion over a second living
summary; an optional compatibility pointer, if the coordinator chooses one,
must contain no substantive state and must not recreate a mandatory turnover
route. Historical references should continue to mean the source they examined,
not silently resolve to an unrelated new brief.

Do not make the optional brief the sole locator for active assignments, local-only
evidence, missing outcomes or uncertain writers. Do not require a final RETURN
before recovering useful saved work. Do not turn completed documentary review
into qualification of real suspension/network loss/process control. These are
the principal ways the thin-brief arrangement could fail its stated purpose.
