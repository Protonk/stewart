## Limits made more precise by the code

The observer computes and emits before/after hashes for the selected sources, review, predictions, construction, six specimens and itself. It raises on a pinned-source mismatch before loading the subject, and on unequal endpoint hashes after the evaluations. It explicitly reports `continuous_preservation_established: false`. Ordinary exceptions during loading, decoding or output terminate the program; independent capture must retain partial output and stderr/status.

The code does **not** compare the authored input hashes to constants from the selected return. It records their starting hashes and checks endpoint equality. An initially different but unchanged review, specimen, prediction or observer could therefore evade that endpoint check. The future arrangement must independently bind all those actual files to the selected identities before execution. The per-specimen digest helps identify the raw specimen actually parsed, but is not by itself an admission of a changed specimen. This clarifies the previously stated identity-mapping requirement; it is not evidence that such a substitution occurred.

There is also a separate read between each source's digest check and `load_selected` loading its path, and between the initial review/specimen hashes and their later reads. Nothing here establishes continuous preservation or proves that all later reads see the checked bytes. Endpoint equality cannot repair that gap. A controlled execution directory, explicit loading/cache conditions and the independently arranged preservation witness must support whatever preservation claim the later result makes. I have not inspected an actual runtime environment, interpreter, cache state or witness, and do not claim these controls already exist.

The before record is emitted before pinned-source checking; seeing it alone would not prove the subject loaded or all evaluations completed. Likewise, a specimen record is an observation from that run, not a verdict that its predicted behavior occurred correctly. The external collector must retain the full ordered output, stderr and final process status, including failures. Parse problems deliberately returned by the subject are data; they are distinct from an observer import/decoding/output failure.

These are bounded execution conditions, not a reason to silently repair or replace the observer, change the specimens, or demand actual wrapper/release authenticity outside the question.
