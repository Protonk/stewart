## The dependency boundary that readers maintained

The exact observer was author-created support for inspecting the proposed
executable route. Its restoration allowed r02 to inspect whether parse problems
were actually emitted, whether reviewed cases were explicitly supplied, which
files were loaded and what the preservation checks did. The reader retained
the distinction between static code inspection and observed execution.

All three readers required future execution arrangements: map the returned
objects to exact staged filenames and identities, select a suitable explicit
interpreter and working directory, bound the six evaluations and effects, name
preservation/loading controls and an independent witness, and retain stdout,
stderr, status and partial output. These were classified as a new execution
undertaking rather than missing construction results. The proposed command's
original author paths are not a portable, ready-to-run environment or a grant
to read those locations.

The observer hardcodes three subject pins but merely fingerprints the authored
inputs and itself at endpoints. It also reopens paths after hashing. The readers
therefore required independent binding of the staged author artifacts and an
honest account of preservation/loading conditions. r02 made the cache/loading
limitation more explicit after seeing the code. These are conditions on any
later run, not evidence that a substitution or failed execution occurred here.
No reader requested broader wrapper behavior, actual release authenticity or
proof of the hypothetical operands' real-world truth to complete this inspection.
