# Auditor form enforcement and prompting

Proposal for the user's separate discussion, 2026-10-29. No enforcement choice
in this briefing is adopted merely by recording it. The user has accepted open
inquiry, inquiry framing and comparison as the initial families, and a
deterministic Markdown rendering generated at preparation and sealed with the
structured answer. [CONTRACTS.md](../../CONTRACTS.md) owns those decisions. Concrete
family specimens remain to be specified; no runtime or model tests ran here.

The remaining issue is what Stewart does when an auditor does not follow the
form: refuse an edit or submission, describe an omission, or prompt for another
turn. These exert different pressure and need separate decisions. Mechanical
intake, custody, exact edit targets, preparation handles, cumulative limits and
manager close already have contracts; this briefing does not reopen them.

## Recommended division

Be strict about whether an entry or reference is mechanically interpretable;
make family expectations concrete and visible without making substantive
completion a condition of sealing. Use one bounded procedural reminder, not
an escalating attempt to obtain a particular kind of answer.

| Situation | Proposed behavior | What this establishes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Malformed call, stale target, unsupported bytes or exceeded limit | Existing contextual refusal; name the field/target, actual effect and ordinary next action | The requested mutation could not be admitted under the operation's contract |
| A structured citation names nonexistent material, an invalid range, or a supplied verbatim quote that differs from the captured bytes | Refuse that entry mutation with a precise diagnostic; preserve the attempted call under the raw-capture contract | The asserted locator/quotation did not match; no conclusion about the argument |
| A locator and quotation match but the evidence does not support the conclusion | Record the attributed answer without a support verdict | The auditor made the claim with this cited basis; matching bytes prove no entailment |
| An expected answer section has no recorded entry | Show a factual omission during preparation; do not refuse submission on that basis | There is no entry in that part of the form; not that no relevant work or evidence exists |
| The auditor reports inadequate material, a poor framing, uncertainty or no supported finding | Provide ordinary authoring and submission paths | The auditor can account for limits without inventing evidence, a defect, a winner or a repair |
| An ordinary model turn ends without submission | At most one procedural reminder across the audit, within remaining limits | Ending a model turn did not seal the answer |

Validate structured citations only. Do not scrape prose for citation-like strings,
rewrite quotations, silently move a locator to matching text, or interpret a
supplied test report as execution Stewart witnessed. Read/search tools should
return usable captured-material references so that correct citation does not
require guessing byte offsets. Exact quote checks concern explicitly verbatim
quotes; paraphrases are attributed prose, not failed exact quotes.

A refused edit must say whether the answer changed and how to correct the call;
when validation refused it, no new answer element exists. The raw attempted call
is still operating evidence, not an accepted answer member. Storage or recording
failures use the existing exceptional-state contract, not a fabricated validation
result or a demand that the auditor repair private state.

## What the form can require

Small per-entry schemas can require interpretable arguments and proper targets.
The recommendation does not make every expected family section mandatory at
submit. An omission check can count entries; it cannot establish that a prose
entry really answered the question. A generic apology should not receive a
Stewart-issued substantive completeness grade any more than an elaborate answer.

Examples of instructions, still to be instantiated in the concrete forms:

- Open inquiry asks for the account supported by the examined material and its
  limits. It does not require a positive finding or universal assurance.
- Inquiry framing asks for the working question, its relation to the concern,
  and the investigation and limitations. It permits explaining why a useful
  bounded investigation could not be made.
- Comparison asks for the alternatives, criterion and evidence bearing on their
  comparison. Unequal evidence, an unavailable criterion or an inability to rank
  the alternatives can be the account; selecting a winner is not required.

Preparation presents a concise snapshot of the selection and mechanically
observable omissions alongside access to the sealed-to-be rendering. For example,
"No comparison entries are recorded" is defensible; "You have not investigated
the alternatives adequately" is not. Observations are ordinary preparation
information, not repeated escalating warnings, separate acknowledgments or a
mandatory inspection pause. The existing confirmed submit is the only effectful
acknowledgment. Damaged selections and unverifiable bytes still refuse under the
custody contract; they are not intellectual omissions.

**The explicit fork is whether an incomplete or empty answer may seal.** The
provisional answer contract currently admits an empty answer. Recommend retaining
that allowance while making the prepared selection clear. A completely empty
answer remains visibly empty, with the actual seal actor; it is never rendered
as "no issues found." This tolerates unhelpful answers. The alternative is to
require an entry for every family obligation, accepting a stated limitation as
an entry. That would strengthen procedural coverage but can incentivize filler,
invented support and loops over how to express an inability. Choosing it would
need an explicit departure from the current empty-answer rule and concrete
coverage schemas; it cannot arrive through a renderer or test fixture.

## When Stewart speaks again

The form's initial instructions explain what work it asks for, how to state
limits and how to prepare and submit. They do not promise a tool-determined test
that the intellectual work is finished.

Proposed single reminder after an ordinary unsubmitted stop, if allowance remains:

> This turn ended without a submission. Your recorded answer remains unsealed.
> If you intend to submit it, use prepare and confirm its handle with submit.

The reminder says nothing about the quality, length or conclusion of the answer.
It permits the normal next response; it does not compel immediate submission or
grant another budget. If the auditor stops again without submitting, the placement
ends with unsubmitted work under the existing status/close rules. The reminder
allowance belongs to the audit and does not reset on continuation. Provider
failures, interrupted tool calls and hard limits are not ordinary stops to which
this reminder should be indiscriminately applied.

Also propose one factual near-limit notice tied to a configured threshold and
the allowance Stewart actually accounts for. It reports the remaining allowance
and that an unsealed answer remains unsubmitted when it is exhausted. It does not
say to find something quickly, claim adequate coverage, or demand a hurried
verdict. Notices and subsequent model work consume the existing budget; no new
allowance appears. Exact threshold and delivery mechanism are engineering choices
after this interaction policy is selected.

At the limit, preserve the recorded work and report the actual unsubmitted state.
No automatic auditor seal, invented conclusion or repeated coaxing occurs. Manager
close remains available to seal the current recorded work with manager attribution.

## Evidence owed if adopted

Use a valid citation, a locator/quote mismatch and a valid but irrelevant citation
to distinguish mechanical checking from semantic grading. Include incomplete and
empty selections, explicit limitations, incompatible auditor claims, and an
ordinary complete response. Verify that refusal preserves the attempted call
without creating an answer element, and that prepare's omissions do not become
submission gates. Exercise reminders before and after continuation, exhausted
allowances, provider errors and repeated ordinary stops. Inspect the exact
orientation and notices delivered. Real-model conformance asks whether models
can use these procedures; agreement with a preferred finding is not its criterion.

The recommendation's tradeoff is deliberate: Stewart teaches through the entry
points, examples, form and preparation feedback, while the auditor can still
return an incomplete or poor answer. It does not certify intellectual completion.
