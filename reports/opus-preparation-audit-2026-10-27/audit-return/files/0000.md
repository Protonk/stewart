## Answer and implication

**The proposed leg can preserve the decided obligations without implementing the deferred physical stop/removal service. No contradictory promise requiring that service is established by the supplied documents.** Adopt this as a bounded implementation direction, subject to making its already-required interface, responsibility and finite acceptance choices concrete before dispatch. The note itself is explicitly a recommendation, not an approved commission (NEXT-LEG.md 3–6, 25–48).

The decisive separation is between fixing the recorded answer and terminating execution. CONTRACTS.md 428–437 permits collect to end authoring without prior cessation or agreement and says, “Collection does not itself establish physical process termination or authorize environment removal or custody close.” A fixed, protected selection can be copied and independently read while source custody remains held. NEXT-LEG.md 18–23 preserves that distinction and supplies a legitimate no-deletion test of source independence. It does not promise a closed audit at this leg's end.

There are material mechanism and qualification tasks to close in the commission: the C-to-audit binding and custody of owed context; integration-owned operation references exposed through supported participant re-entry; and verified delivery with dependency-specific gaps. They are within the proposed leg, not reasons to restore lifecycle stages or require a general control service. Actual access protection cannot be deferred merely because live launch/control is excluded. If the chosen adapter cannot enforce the promised boundary or support the named re-entry route, the coordinator must narrow its supported claims or return that concrete problem for scope decision.

The genuinely unsettled policy is disposal when selection identity is unknowable. It need not be settled to deliver independently verifiable material with explicit limitations and retain the unresolved undertaking. NEXT-LEG.md 39–44 calls its no-close treatment a proposed deferral, not permanent policy. That remains an appropriate explicit disposition for this leg; no warning acknowledgment, delivered copy or lost reply supplies disposal authority.

### 1. Bind the original undertaking and preserve the finite owed context

**Sequence.** C accepts commission A; a new audit is opened against it; recorded answer edits succeed. Before result copying, the C bundle becomes unavailable at its retained locator. A design retaining only that locator and no recoverable context would be unable to supply the original question/instructions. Separately, a locator without an independent identity cannot distinguish A from a self-consistent replacement bundle; a live-source fallback could supply later-edited instructions. The first affected obligation is original-commission binding/context custody, before answer delivery. Delivering every answer artifact would not repair missing context: RETURN-COMPLETENESS 37 calls that package incomplete. This does not imply that every later loss is preventable; the service must preserve its original binding and diagnose unavailable owed bytes honestly.

**Nearby valid cases.** Later edits to the manager's original source must leave the recorded commission invariant. Two distinct accepted commissions or collections remain distinct even with identical question or answer bytes. Damage to an unselected, unneeded dependency must not invalidate an otherwise verifiable finite return. The return is not required to copy every commission input by implication (RETURN-COMPLETENESS 13–18); C's manager-custodied input bundle remains a separate responsibility.

**Smallest clarification.** Before dispatch, specify the independent original reference, the exact question/instruction/receipt dependencies the new service undertakes to retain, when that undertaking starts, and the supported reader for each. Snapshot and verify owed bytes through the admitted boundary, or explicitly account for a failed intake; never substitute live originals. This is an implementation choice within NEXT-LEG 11 and 31–38, not a new retention or replay policy. Extend its fresh-manager and source-independent cases at 66–72 to remove original context access as well as answer custody, and compare a changed live source with an unrelated damaged member. Retain an external witness.

### 2. Qualify interruption at the participant boundary, including unresolved selection races

**Sequence.** The integration dispatches add with reference R. Consider separately a cut before backend intent publication, after element publication but before reply, and during delivery of the reply. The caller or wrapper is gone. If the returning auditor only sees “try add again” without R, it cannot distinguish the already-published addition from an intentional new equal-byte addition. The first lost distinction is operation identity at dispatch/re-entry, not the presence of bytes in storage. A still earlier uninitialized-store cut is expressly outside the prototype's verified barriers (PROTOTYPE-RESULT 118–122).

**Nearby valid cases.** A new R2 may intentionally add identical bytes; unrelated element readback remains useful. An unverifiable R must retain its uncertainty, not be inferred absent. AUTHORING-BOUNDARY 42–47 requires pre-dispatch ownership and rejects guessing by source, name, equal bytes or recency. Its 60–65 limits the candidate promise to visibility during a continuing call or supported return; “A ledger cannot wake an absent auditor.”

Now let manager collect race with an unresolved add or confirmed submit. The concrete write protocol must account for the add's actual membership boundary, bind one fixed selection, and preserve the winning submit/collect attribution. Staging bytes alone cannot become a member; a lost reply cannot erase one. A verifiably empty selection remains an honest valid return; unknown membership cannot become empty. INTERACTION-SIMPLIFICATIONS 163–168 leaves detailed unfinished-add treatment for design, so this audit does not invent a particular arbitration algorithm.

**Smallest clarification.** Name who keeps R before dispatch, how a participant returns to it after wrapper/caller loss, the no-effect/published/unverifiable outcomes, and how collect handles pending edits without silently discarding their obligations. Define which failed initialization cuts are supported and their safe next action. Include the above cuts, intentional equal-byte addition, unrelated readback, pending-add/collect race, and repeated winning/losing finalization calls in NEXT-LEG 61–65's finite population. Observe the actual participant interface separately from internal records. No process launcher, automatic audit restart, eventual wake-up guarantee or new resolve/resume/cancel stage is necessary; actual qualification still needs an authorized route/trial.

### 3. Make delivery completion, damage and access separate observable facts

**Sequence.** One submitted or manager-collected return is fixed. Destination copying fails after a partial output; alternatively all bytes and completion evidence are written but the reply disappears. A fresh manager returns through help/status. A service that treats both as “copy absent,” starts another collection, or treats the earlier request as close authority loses respectively the copy-outcome distinction, fixed return identity, or the manager's post-receipt decision. RETURN-COMPLETENESS 35–36 states these separate cases; CONTRACTS 415–417 rejects turning a lost copy reply into removal permission.

**Nearby valid cases.** Complete source custody with failed destination delivery differs from missing/corrupt selected bytes. Known member loss differs from an invalid manifest/provenance record that prevents knowing what is owed. An unused preparation or unrelated attempt may be damaged while the required selection remains verifiable. Known empty membership differs from unknown inventory. A manager moving or losing its copy later does not rewrite the earlier delivery observation (RETURN-COMPLETENESS 58–60).

**Smallest clarification.** Specify the destination selected at commissioning, copy-operation identity and ownership of partial outputs, independent reader, and supported normal continuation or explicit unresolved account. Preserve stable diagnostics and useful verified access for each required dependency; never label partial delivery complete or require private-state repair. Include missing original question/commission binding alongside payload and manifest damage. NEXT-LEG 66–72 already covers the general categories; these make the distinctions measurable. Make audit custody unavailable to the reader and retain the witness outside affected custody as 21–23 requires. This establishes only the tested source-independence property, not power-loss durability or successful future removal.

Actual grants are part of this same boundary. CONTRACTS 300–303 and 506–519 require mediated answer changes and qualified access, including shell/file routes; manager return access must not reopen live material. A cooperative-driver lock or written grant is not that evidence. The selected integration can qualify authorized editing, denied direct custody mutation/unselected access, and manager access to the fixed return without implementing process control. If no such route is available, product-access qualification remains explicitly unestablished; a passing process suite cannot replace it.

### Close/control deferral and the remaining policy question

An external auditor process may still run after collect. With the actual protected tool boundary, subsequent add/replace/delete/submit calls cannot change the fixed answer; repeated copying stays bound to that answer. This is a useful invariance case for sealing, and makes no claim that the process or its descendants stopped. Removing its environment would be another act requiring the deferred control evidence (CONTRACTS 954–961). The proposed route never needs to perform that act.

Similarly, superseded bytes, holds, failure evidence and work outside the helper need not all become members of the selected return. Their separate obligations cannot silently be declared extinguished. CONTRACTS 76–99, 327–331 and RETURN-COMPLETENESS 50–57 preserve those distinctions. A close-free implementation can keep recorded material and expose relevant unfinished custody; it must not advertise workspace capture or fulfillment of outside-helper preservation promises it has not implemented. The narrow package definition supplies no disposal authority over that work.

Known-selection incomplete closing has accepted authority subject to naming the expected identity, observed failure, delivered material and obligation ended (CONTRACTS 389–400). Unknown selection cannot support an exact missing list; its disposal authority is genuinely undecided. The smallest current scope disposition is the candidate's explicit retention of available material/diagnostics and unfinished close. If a later commission promises to finish such closure, that named policy decision and removal/control evidence become prerequisites. They are not prerequisites for this bounded answer-and-copy route.
