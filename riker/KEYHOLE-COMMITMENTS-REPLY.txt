The decisive boundary is not whether Stewart has received useful information, but whether it has undertaken to preserve that information.

A successful setter call does not inherently establish durable custody. It can mean only that a field was staged successfully in a disposable authoring draft. Equally, a rejected final submission need not establish custody merely because the service inspected its contents.

But a general declaration that drafts are disposable cannot override a more specific representation that material has been saved, recorded, or accepted for preservation. The relevant question is what a reasonable auditor is entitled to rely on after each acknowledged operation.

Both candidate boundaries can be coherent. They create different obligations, however, and neither can silently inherit the existing promise to recover partial work after graceless close.

## 1. Admission-only custody

Under the first boundary, setter success means:

> The field was staged successfully in the live authoring draft. It is available for continued authoring while that draft exists, but has not entered durable custody and will not be recovered after authorized disposal.

A successful setter therefore creates a specific, limited undertaking: Stewart has performed the stated staging operation. It has not necessarily undertaken preservation.

At rejected final submission, the interface should communicate two facts independently: the submitted answer failed schema admission, and no durable return or preserved attempt was created. It should not merely say that a required value was invalid while leaving custody ambiguous.

On graceless close, the audit has no accepted return. The historical record can say that the audit ended without a valid report and, where recorded, that an attempt failed admission. It must not fabricate a `needs-evidence` report or imply that the partial text was preserved.

This boundary can be honest. But it explicitly abandons recovery of partially authored work, including useful text previously handled by Stewart's tools.

## 2. Raw custody at final attempt

The second boundary makes the same limited promise at setter success. The difference begins when the auditor attempts final submission.

At that point, the interface promises:

> The bounded attempted submission has been durably recorded. Schema admission failed, so it is not an accepted audit return. The recorded attempt remains recoverable independently of the authoring environment.

This creates reliance on preservation of a particular received object, not on preservation of the working environment.

The crucial distinction is between two independent outcomes:

- Custody: Was the attempted payload durably preserved?
- Admission: Did that payload satisfy the required schema?

A schema rejection can coexist with successful custody. Conversely, failure to preserve an attempted payload cannot be reported as merely a schema rejection if durable receipt was promised.

After graceless close, the manager can retrieve the unadmitted attempt, associated with its commission and clearly distinguished from an accepted answer. No guarantee of correctness, completeness, or usefulness follows.

Importantly, this does not preserve setter drafts that were never submitted.

## 3. The strongest counterexample

Consider a slightly sharper version of the proposed scenario.

The auditor uses the supplied setters to enter several substantial findings. Each setter reports success.

Final submission then supplies only a required discriminator, misspelled as `anwer` rather than `answer`. The previously entered text still resides in the service's mutable draft.

The service rejects the submission. The manager requests graceless close.

Now suppose Stewart preserves the raw final tool invocation, but not the assembled attempted answer. It has retained the misspelled discriminator while losing every substantive finding.

This exposes a weakness even in the second boundary: preserving a final-attempt event is not necessarily preserving the attempted submission.

If raw attempt custody is promised, its object must be defined as the complete bounded payload assembled for submission, including the previously set fields. Merely logging the final operation's arguments would not fulfill that undertaking.

There is also a counterexample to the first boundary's reliance language.

Suppose setter responses say saved, while a general commission notice says that scratch work is disposable. The auditor reasonably relies on the more specific successful storage representations, does not create an independent copy, and then loses the text after schema rejection and early close.

The general notice may not adequately qualify those representations. The problem is not that valuable information was lost. It is that the interface may have induced reliance on its retention.

This is why advance disposable-draft terms are necessary but not always sufficient. They must agree with the semantics communicated at the successful tool operation.

## 4. The smallest clear commitment

I would distinguish the smallest contractually coherent boundary from the smallest positive preservation commitment.

If Stewart is prepared to abandon recovery of unadmitted partial answers, admission-only custody is sufficient. Its essential commitment is an unambiguous three-way distinction between ephemeral staging, unsuccessful admission without custody, and successful durable admission.

No tool response should imply broader persistence.

If Stewart wants a meaningful partial-recovery promise at the final handoff, the smallest positive commitment is custody of the complete bounded attempted submission before schema admission. That requires neither raw capture of every setter operation nor preservation of arbitrary working files.

I favor the latter boundary if the intent is to preserve a defensible remnant of the existing early-close promise. It protects work the auditor has affirmatively attempted to hand over, while still allowing unsubmitted drafts and unrelated scratch artifacts to disappear.

It is nevertheless a policy choice, not a consequence of the mere fact that the service saw the data.

## 5. Existing process promises

| Existing promise                                                             | Required treatment                                                                                                                                                                    |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Raw capture before interpretation                                            | Explicitly narrow its scope. Under admission-only custody, it applies to admitted results. Under attempt custody, it applies to bounded final attempts before admission judgment.     |
| Disposition of every gap                                                     | Replace any universal obligation to inventory discarded scratch. Continue accounting for missing records, incomplete operations, and other gaps within the promised custody boundary. |
| Attribution without verdict                                                  | Retain. Identify the auditor, commission, recorded return or attempt, and relevant status without treating structural admission as intellectual validation.                           |
| Obtainable partial work after early/graceless close                          | Explicitly revoke under the first boundary. Under the second, narrow it to received and durably preserved attempts, not arbitrary unfinished work.                                    |
| Original commission and outcome custody                                      | Retain for accepted results, including preservation of their association with the original commission.                                                                                |
| Recoverability after a lost success reply                                    | Retain without qualification. An admitted, durably recorded result remains that same result regardless of whether its acknowledgment arrived.                                         |
| Holds, incomplete custody operations, safe removal, and verified termination | Retain independently of the new deliverable boundary.                                                                                                                                 |

There is one additional distinction worth protecting.

The proposed automatic retirement condition requires durable valid final submission and verified termination. In the counterexample, no valid final submission exists. Consequently, that successful-completion retirement condition has not been met.

An early-close path might independently authorize retirement of a disposable draft, but that authority must be established separately. Absence of a preservation duty is not, by itself, authority to remove an environment.

Likewise, an admitted submission whose acknowledgment was lost cannot be treated as an unadmitted attempt on retry. Durable custody, not the fate of the reply, determines the recorded result.

## Conclusion

The proposition I would challenge is that successful interaction with an authoring tool is equivalent to undertaking durable preservation.

It is not. But successful interaction does create reliance on whatever operation the interface represents as having occurred.

Stewart can make disposable drafts legitimate by specifying that setter success commits only ephemeral state and that schema rejection creates no durable result. To make that boundary credible, it must expressly withdraw the current partial-work recovery promise and avoid contradictory success language.

If Stewart instead chooses to preserve unadmitted final attempts, its narrow obligation is to preserve the entire attempted answer as assembled at handoff, not just evidence that a submission was attempted.

Neither approach requires retaining every valuable artifact. Both require Stewart to distinguish work it was authorized to lose from material it specifically undertook to keep.
