The most consequential remaining design work, to my eye, concerns
how Stewart acquires an answer, makes it available, and completes its custody
operations when something goes wrong. I would give those interactions a more
definite shape before adding conveniences for restarting investigations.

## 1. Keep the executor removal; choose stable publication as the capture boundary

Run 009 selects no dedicated Stewart program executor while retaining auditor
tool use. The remaining capture choice includes a proposal to require supervised
session exit and exclusion of writers. The [closing plan](../runs/run-009/context/berman/closing-project/DEVELOPMENT-PLAN.md#opening-decision-round-a-narrow-custodial-core)
also carries the intended accommodation of human auditors and varied launch
arrangements.

I favor making an identified, stable publication the central capture condition.
Process exit can help establish that condition on a supported harness route;
a human auditor could instead hand over a fixed version of a return package.
Stewart would acquire that version and record its identity, supplied claims and
capture outcome. Failed investigative work could still produce an entirely
usable return: a report explaining the failure and its supporting material.

This would require a concrete way to freeze or publish the selected material.
A human's working directory or an agent's assertion that it has finished would
not itself supply the stronger stability promise. For an interrupted audit,
Stewart could capture an explicitly partial result with the available evidence.
The interface would distinguish that result from acquisition of a completed
publication.

This choice gives the different auditor arrangements a common product boundary
without making Stewart their general supervisor. It also keeps a real duty with
Stewart: acquiring a particular return reliably. I would accept the work of
supporting that boundary while continuing to omit the staged script approval,
release and invocation service. The [execution comparison](../runs/run-009/notes/017-comparing-execution-and-authorization.txt)
makes the cost of the omission clear: Stewart gives up its own program-process
witness. I think that is a worthwhile trade for this app.

## 2. Prefer zero audit restarts and strong recovery of identified custody operations

The [recorded execution decision](../runs/run-009/notes/018-no-executor-decision-and-coordinator-handoff.txt)
leaves zero versus one audit-restart convenience open. It also retains
responsibility for interrupted capture, publication and close. I would choose
zero audit restarts for the first successor and put the interface effort into
recovering those operations. The plan already identifies existing request
bindings and closing records as design material; their ability to identify
unfinished work is worth carrying into the smaller app.

Consider a manager that requests export, loses the reply, and asks again.
Stewart should identify the original export and say whether its product exists,
which steps remain, and what completion is possible. Repeating that identified
request should resolve the same undertaking. Resampling a changed audit directory
would create a different observation and deserves a different identity.

For close, the same principle becomes quite concrete: continue from the frozen
removal inventory. If a path now contains a replacement directory, the recovery
route must account for that mismatch instead of treating the new directory as
the old target. Run 008's investigations and the subsequent
[close decisions](../runs/run-009/context/berman/closing-project/DEVELOPMENT-PLAN.md#2-audit-refusals-and-existing-custody-responsibilities)
already make judged contents and frozen identities central.

I would make the original operation's identity available before its first
effect, so a lost reply leaves the manager a way to find it again. The manager
should be able to inspect and complete or explicitly dispose of the undertaking
through Stewart. This is complexity worth retaining: it directly addresses the
researcher's concern that an agent-manager will otherwise improvise repairs in
private state. A restart button for the whole audit would offer much less help
with these particular failures.

## 3. Make an available answer easy to read while custody remains open

The selected hold arrangement permits retirement of the working environment
while the audit retains identified captures; final custody close waits for
release. I support that arrangement. I would make the ordinary return interface
express its consequences directly: the answer can be available while material
is held or cleanup remains incomplete. A hold should have no implication that
the auditor is still investigating.

There is a related availability decision in run 008. An intact selected report
could become unexportable after damage to a sibling runtime capture, even though
an earlier report-only package remained usable. The
[comparison account](../runs/run-008/notes/2026-10-23-custody-comparisons-and-boundaries.txt)
records the case; the later plan accepts selected-report export with the sibling
damage carried explicitly.

I would apply that selection-based approach to the ordinary return reader too.
It should show the verified report, the evidence supporting the selected view,
and relevant surrounding damage. This extends the recorded export decision into
the product's usual answer-reading path. Closing the whole audit would still
evaluate the material and obligations within that operation's scope.

For example, after a successful capture and a failed retirement, the manager
could receive the answer together with the identity of the unfinished custody
operation. It would have a usable result and a supported way to resolve the
remaining work. Making the manager wait for every cleanup step, or requiring a
new accepted-answer acknowledgment, would add friction to a relationship meant
to deliver a bounded answer. The archive already records declining the latter
receipt. The [hold discussion](../runs/run-009/notes/005-holds-and-the-product-relationship.txt)
gives a better foundation: inquiry completion and final custody close have
different conditions.

## 4. Prefer a plain subject directory and a separately granted history view

For the open workspace and history decisions, I favor one ordinary subject-tree
representation: a materialized directory, with historical inspection supplied
through a separately selected, pinned, read-only view. A history-only audit
would receive that view and a return area, without requiring a subject tree.

This fits the [selected access direction](../runs/run-009/notes/007-selected-access-direction-and-remaining-recovery.txt):
Stewart can describe the material it supplied instead of maintaining an expanding
inventory of everything in the manager's environment that must stay private.
It also permits rich context. A narrow question can justify extensive history;
the material selection should express that purpose rather than default to the
smallest possible context.

I would initially pay the copying cost for the selected history. The
[archived history proposal account](../runs/run-009/context/berman/closing-project/DEVELOPMENT-PLAN.md#opening-decision-round-a-narrow-custodial-core)
identifies the problem with borrowing the subject repository's object store:
an auditor can reach objects beyond the apparent selected view. Shared immutable
storage remains attractive internally, but its access boundary has to preserve
the actual selection.

I prefer this to a live history-query service because the latter introduces an
interface operating during the audit, with its own availability, authority and
query-recording responsibilities. A supplied history view keeps investigation
local to the granted material. The practical cost is that Git-dependent tools
expecting a normal checkout may need a different invocation or may not fit this
workspace form. That cost belongs in the app's supported audit capabilities. I
would accept it rather than preserve the whole worktree lifecycle by default.

## 5. Give the narrowed reader a specific first consumer: the recipient of an exported result

Run 009 replaces blanket compatibility with Python's operational records with
supported interpretation for named consumers and record populations. I support
that narrowing and would choose a concrete first consumer: a manager or later
investigator opening an exported result after its original audit environment
and Python installation are gone.

The reader should recover the question, selected subject identity, report,
supporting captured material, collection identity and recorded qualifications.
It should preserve the distinction between a process result reported by the
auditor and bytes or events Stewart itself observed. Equal report bytes from
different collections should remain separately attributable.

The product case is an exported result moved to another directory or machine,
with no original working tree, manager launcher or writer available. The
[earlier launcher-damage comparison revisited in 009](../runs/run-009/notes/009-reader-purpose-and-inherited-commitment.txt)
shows why this consumer matters: inspection had acquired a dependency on
operational command rendering. I would make the portable result the reader's
primary object and keep any necessary diagnosis of unfinished Python lifetimes
as a separately scoped transition capability.

This also gives the hold design a concrete beneficiary. Preserving a finite
selection is more useful when that selection carries the material needed to
understand the custody claims made about it. A held bundle that can be read only
by reconstructing the retired operational environment would retain much of the
dependency the recent decisions are trying to remove.
